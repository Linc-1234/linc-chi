What Is 3D Design?

Understanding the foundation of three-dimensional creation.

Digital Objects

3D design allows creators to build digital representations of objects, characters, environments and spaces with height, width and depth.

Multiple Angles

Unlike a flat drawing, a 3D model can be viewed, rotated, modified and studied from different angles.

Real-World Applications

3D technology is used in architecture, engineering, product design, games, film, education and advertising.

Core Geometry

The fundamental building blocks of 3D models.

01 / VERTICES

Vertices

A vertex is a point in three-dimensional space. Multiple vertices can be connected to create model structures.

02 / EDGES

Edges

An edge connects two vertices and helps define the shape of a polygonal mesh.

03 / FACES

Faces

Faces are surfaces enclosed by edges. Together, vertices, edges and faces form polygonal models.

04 / MESH

Mesh

A mesh is a collection of connected geometric elements representing the surface of a 3D object.

05 / TOPOLOGY

Topology

Topology describes how vertices, edges and faces are arranged within a model.

06 / AXES

X, Y & Z

Three-dimensional scenes use coordinate axes to control position, rotation and scale.

Types of 3D Modelling

Different modelling methods are useful for different projects.

Wireframe Modelling

Focuses on the skeletal structure of an object using vertices and edges.

Surface Modelling

Focuses on creating the outer surfaces of objects, often used for complex shapes and product visualization.

Solid Modelling

Represents objects as complete volumes and is common in engineering and manufacturing.

Digital Sculpting

Allows artists to shape highly detailed digital models in a way similar to traditional sculpting.

Polygonal Modelling

Builds objects from connected polygon faces and is widely used in games, animation and visual effects.

Parametric Design

Uses editable dimensions, relationships and parameters to control designs.

The 3D Design Workflow

A typical journey from an idea to a finished visual.

01 — CONCEPT

Concept

Start with an idea, sketch, reference image or design requirement.

02 — BLOCKOUT

Blockout

Use simple shapes to establish proportions, composition and scale.

03 — MODELLING

Modelling

Refine geometry by adding details and adjusting surfaces.

04 — TEXTURING

UV & Texturing

Prepare surfaces for colours, patterns, materials and details.

05 — LIGHTING

Lighting & Camera

Position lights and cameras to control realism, mood and composition.

06 — RENDER

Rendering

Generate the final image, animation or visual output.

Materials, Textures & Lighting

Turning geometry into believable surfaces.

Materials

Materials describe how surfaces interact with light. They can represent metal, glass, plastic, wood, stone and more.

Textures

Textures add colours, patterns, surface details and signs of wear to digital models.

Lighting

Lighting creates highlights, shadows, depth and mood within a 3D scene.

Rendering

Rendering converts the 3D scene into a final visual output such as an image or animation.

Where Is 3D Design Used?

Industries using 3D technology around the world.

Architecture

Buildings, interiors and environments can be visualized before construction begins.

Engineering

Engineers create and evaluate components, products and mechanical systems.

Film & Television

3D models and visual effects can create characters, creatures, environments and digital effects.

Video Games

Games use 3D assets for characters, vehicles, buildings, environments and objects.

Product Design

Designers can visualize and refine products before physical production.

Education & Science

3D models can make complex scientific concepts, machines and structures easier to understand.
